How the pieces fit

The model has four nested levels you work through, plus two things that sit beside them. Levels are not a layer in the tree: each capability area is placed at one level (Table 2, p. 85).

The hierarchy
Example artifacts
Listed per capability area as evidence. They are examples only and map many-to-many: one artifact can serve several areas (p. 32).
Maturity indicators
Accountability, Planning and Resourcing (Section 4.13, pp. 81–83). They decide what level a capability area is actually operating at.
Context attributes
A profile of the organizational unit (Table 1, p. 27). Not scored. They decide which practices are “applicable” and what to prioritize.
How a level is reached
  • 1Goals are required. All goals and the applicable practices of a capability area must be done for its dimension to reach that area’s level.
  • 2Levels are cumulative. Table 2 lists the minimum areas for each level. Level 3 needs every Level 1, 2 and 3 area.
  • 3Indicators cap the rating. A capability area is rated at its lowest maturity indicator, even if all goals and practices are met (p. 87).
  • 4Weakest link wins. A dimension is rated at its lowest capability area (p. 87).
  • 5All areas, all indicators. On top of the Table 2 areas, every capability area is expected to meet the indicators of the target level (p. 84).
